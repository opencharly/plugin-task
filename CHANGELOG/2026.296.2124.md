# 2026.296.2124 — feat(git-submodules): bound NEGLECT in currency mode with an opt-in `stale_after`

## Summary

`verb:git-submodules` in `mode: currency` answers ONE question — *is each recorded pin the ref its
intent names?* — and it answers it correctly. What it could not answer is the question a repo
that **owns** the pin needs: *how long has this repo itself neglected to advance it?*

This PR adds that answer as an **opt-in** knob, `stale_after`, on the currency mode.

**The defect it closes, measured.** From `opencharly/charly`'s own advance history against each
`box/*` upstream's commit dates, 2026-09-22 → 2026-10-23 (behind-time as the **union** of the
behind-intervals, not the sum of overlapping per-commit lags):

| twin | behind-time | share of the window | continuous behind-windows | max continuous behind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `box/arch` | 554 h | **73.7 %** | 4 | 237 h |
| `box/cachyos` | 589 h | **78.4 %** | 5 | 230 h |
| `box/debian` | 566 h | **75.4 %** | 4 | 238 h |
| `box/fedora` | 564 h | **75.0 %** | 4 | 237 h |
| `box/ubuntu` | 578 h | **77.0 %** | 4 | 238 h |
| `box/omarchy` | 371 h | 49.4 % | 4 | 196 h |

Two regimes hide in that table and must not be confused:

* **CHURN** — a twin whose upstream moves daily is *behind* between every wave. That is the normal
  state of a healthy tree.
* **NEGLECT** — what actually recurred was the owner's **advance cadence**: gaps of **8 days**
  (10-06 → 10-16) and **10 days** (09-28 → 10-06) with no advance at all, against a *healthy*
  maximum gap of **5 days** (10-16 → 10-21). Every twin's own bookkeeping age is ≤ 1 day today.

**The criterion that was first proposed, and why it is not this one.** The RCA filed as
opencharly/charly#1048 proposed: *fail when a twin is behind the ref its own `.gitmodules`
declares.* The table above falsifies it — that gate would be red roughly three days out of four on
a perfectly healthy tree, and the org's own pin rule forbids exactly that:

> Only a pin the workflow cannot JUDGE, or two sides that DISAGREE, is a failure; `behind` and a
> `blocked-by` that names a producer leg PASS and are REPORTED … a gate that failed there would be
> permanently red — **which trains every reader to ignore it**, the mirror problem inverted.

So the criterion was re-derived against the measurement, not implemented as written.

**What `stale_after` does.**

* The bound is on the **AGE of the owner's own last commit that touched the pin's path** — never
  on the pin's distance from the served ref. The distance is churn; the age is neglect.
* A pin that is **not current AND has not been advanced within the bound** is **STALE**, and a
  stale pin **FAILS**.
* A **`cur` pin is never stale**, however old its last advance: a quiet upstream is not neglect,
  and no quiet repo may go red on its own silence.
* A **`blocked-by` edge is not eligible**: the producer's neglect is the producer's business, and
  failing here over another repo's PR is the permanently-red trap again.
* **Unset ⇒ off.** The verdict and the report are byte-unchanged, so no repo sees a behaviour
  change until it declares a bound.

**Where it lives, and why.** In the canonical judgement — this verb — with the schema as the single
source (`candy/plugin-task/schema/task.cue` → the regenerated `candy/plugin-task/params/cue_types_gen.go`).
The owner declares the bound in its own manifest; no repo re-derives "is the pin at the tip" for
itself, and no second implementation of the currency judgement appears anywhere.

**The calibration.** The healthy maximum gap between advances was 5 days and the neglect gaps were
8 and 10 days, so a **7-day (168 h)** bound sits above the healthy maximum and below the neglect
minimum, with margin on both sides.

The consumer side — the owner declaring the bound and advancing its twin through
`charly task sync` — is opencharly/charly#1048 and lands separately, producer-first.

same `168h` bound, with only the DATE of the commit that recorded the pin changed —

1. knob unset → **PASS**, and the report contains no `STALE` (the opt-in is real, and the default
   verdict is unchanged);
2. old date (240 h) → **FAIL**, `1 STALE pin(s) (neglected for more than 168h)`, naming the pin,
   its age and the bound;
3. recent date (1 min) → **PASS** with `1 of 2 pin(s) NOT current` still reported — churn, not
   neglect;
4. a `cur` pin with a 240 h old advance → **PASS** — the idle-upstream trap;
5. a malformed bound (`7d`) → **FAIL** with `not a duration` — a broken control is never silent.
